# Get started

### Get started

Picture a small robot turtle parked at (0, 0) on the x-y plane. Once you run `import turtle`, issue the instruction `turtle.forward(15)`; it travels (visibly, on your screen) 15 pixels along whatever heading it currently holds, tracing a line as it goes. Issue `turtle.right(25)` and the turtle pivots where it stands, 25 degrees to the clockwise side.

Within Python, turtle graphics models a tangible ?turtle? ? essentially a tiny pen-carrying robot ? inking a sheet of paper laid out on the floor.

This makes a proven, effective route for newcomers meeting programming ideas and software interaction for the first time, because every action yields immediate, on-screen results. On top of that, it grants handy access to graphical output more broadly.

The turtle system began life as a teaching aid meant for classroom use by instructors. When a developer simply wants a bit of graphical output, it offers a path to that goal that skips the burden of pulling heavier or third-party libraries into the project.
